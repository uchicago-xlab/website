RISK AREA

Nuclear

About

XLab’s nuclear risk research program is motivated by the conceptual gap between the canonical nuclear policy field and the resilience and post-detonation sub-field.

While these areas are often treated as separate and distinct entities, XLab’s program treats these research areas as interdependent, cultivating a robust university community focused on producing actionable policy-relevant research that fills in critical knowledge gaps in our understanding of nuclear preparedness, nuclear winter, and the post-nuclear first strike deterrence environment. Our program functions as a complement to the prevention-based risk reduction frameworks that dominate the field, providing possible solutions to mitigating nuclear risk when preventative frameworks face increased opposition and roadblocks in a tri-polar security environment. XLab’s nuclear risk research focuses on three key areas.

Our Research Focus

Risk Reduction Alternatives

Much of the risk fatigue and funding decreases in the nuclear risk field may be attributed to no return on investment, as despite research, policy initiatives, and calls for reducing nuclear risk, the risk is evolving and continues to rise. Much of the field focuses on prevention but applies Cold War-era approaches for risk reduction to a modern security climate that looks very different, particularly concerning tri-polar competition. We are motivated to understand where current approaches fall flat in modern conditions, and develop alternatives that can work despite a more hostile security environment. This includes revisiting Cold War-era strategies like minimum viable deterrence, and testing them on modern geopolitical conditions.

Updating the Field’s Foundational Inputs

The security environment has evolved drastically since the Cold War, yet the nuclear field often treats today’s landscape as the same. The nuclear field often confuses the ‘politically irrelevant’ with the ‘scientifically resolved.’ When certain questions fall out of political relevance, the field often stops investigating them; it includes public risk narratives, decision-making, and military planning; hard sciences (e.g., effects of nuclear war and nuclear winter). This research area re-interrogates those inputs under modern conditions to identify where real uncertainty lies and where future work is most needed. Our 2026 report on assumptions and uncertainties in nuclear winter modeling, for example, audits the choices embedded at each stage of the nuclear winter mechanism chain, from fuel loading and firestorm behavior, to soot injection and global circulation. It also shows which of them carry the most weight in the field’s estimates. We also build open-source, interactive tools that make these inputs visible and testable.

Right-of-Boom and Resilience

This research area seeks to broaden the definition of what constitutes risk reduction and what may happen if prevention one day fails. While civil defense and other post-detonation interventions were seen as morally suspect and fatalistic during the Cold War, we argue this area of inquiry should be reframed as risk management for a new nuclear era. The refusal to understand and prepare for possible failure modes increases the probability and severity of a failure event itself. This includes nuclear winter research, institutional resilience, and more broadly, what a post first-strike world looks like.

Research and Working Groups

XLab’s research working group program allows students to pursue impactful research over quarter-long project cycles advised by XLab staff. We provide otherwise rare opportunities for students to engage with topics in nuclear risk, familiarize themselves with the research process, and develop critical analysis skills over the course of the project. Each working group produces a journal article-length deliverable. Most research groups run on a quarterly basis, while others are longer-term initiatives. What We’ve Been Up to Lately

People

Madeline Berzak

Director, Nuclear Risk

Directs the nuclear risk research program at XLab. Madeline holds a bachelor’s in political science and public policy from American University, and a master’s in nonproliferation and terrorism studies from the Middlebury Institute of International Studies at Monterey. She was formerly an open source intelligence analyst at the James Martin Center for Nonproliferation Studies, focusing on North Korea’s nuclear weapons program, and has worked for the Nuclear Threat Initiative and Physicians for Social Responsibility. Madeline also runs Appetites for Risk, a Substack on the relationship between big risks and culture.

Rhea Kanuparthi

Manages the emerging technology research program at XLab. Rhea is a fourth-year at the University of Chicago studying political science and mathematics, and leads technical research projects with XLab’s partner organization, the Wisconsin AI Safety Initiative (WAISI). Her research focuses on the impacts of emerging technology on nuclear strategy, deterrence with mutually assured AI malfunction (MAIM), and shifts in crisis dynamics in South Asia. She has worked with XLab since January 2024, and was a summer research fellow in 2024.

Noah Peters

A research assistant for the nuclear risk program at XLab. Noah is an incoming third-year studying mathematics and economics with a concentration in data science. He worked on the Winter 2026 civil defense project, examining the relationship between civil defense and the probability of nuclear war; his broader interests include political and economic resilience, disaster response, post-nuclear war autarky, and export control regimes.

Bennett Cullison

A researcher and lead on the nuclear winter geoengineering project. Bennett is an incoming fourth-year studying geophysical sciences, with research interests in climate and atmospheric modeling, nuclear winter, and GIS applications. He previously worked on XLab’s nuclear winter research, modeling combustible material by land type to produce more accurate soot estimates.

XLab's Approach

HIGH-LEVEL CONVENING

XLab organized the Nobel Laureate Assembly to Prevent Nuclear War, bringing together the world's foremost experts on nuclear weapons and resulting in a Declaration signed by 129 Nobel Laureates.

POLICY RESEARCH

Fellow-led research on escalation dynamics, arms control, and the intersection of emerging technology (including AI) with nuclear command and control.

PUBLIC PROGRAMMING

Panels and screenings held with partners including the Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists, most recently on the annual Doomsday Clock announcement and a screening of Kathryn Bigelow's A House of Dynamite.
